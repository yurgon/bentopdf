How to convert PDF to TIFF

  1. Add your PDF

    One file. It stays on your device.

  2. Set DPI, compression and color

    300 DPI with LZW suits most uses. For fax and e-filing choose Black & White with CCITT Group 4.

  3. Convert and save

    Tick Save as multi-page TIFF for one file, or get a ZIP of page_1.tiff, page_2.tiff and so on.

What you get

Compression options
Compression Type Best for
LZW (default) Lossless Any content
Deflate / ZIP Lossless Any content
CCITT Group 4 Lossless, 1-bit Black and white scans, fax, e-filing
JPEG Lossy, quality 85 Photo-heavy pages
PackBits or None Legacy Older systems

TIFF is the format of scanners, fax systems and document archives, and some agencies and e-filing systems accept only TIFF. Converting PDF to TIFF turns each page into a bitmap image. You set the resolution and compression in your browser, so the document is never uploaded.

One multi-page TIFF or one file per page

Tick Save as multi-page TIFF (single file) and every page is written into one .tiff named after the PDF. Leave it unticked and a multi-page PDF downloads as a ZIP with page_1.tiff, page_2.tiff and so on. A one-page PDF always gives a single .tiff.

Which DPI to use

The slider runs from 72 to 600 and defaults to 300, the usual choice for print and archival scans. Fax and document management systems typically expect 200 to 300, and 150 is enough for screens. Very high DPI on large pages can run the browser out of memory.

Black and white TIFF for fax or e-filing

Set Color Mode to Black & White (1-bit) and Compression to CCITT Group 4, then pick 200 or 300 DPI. That combination is what most fax gateways and document systems expect, and it makes very small files. CCITT Group 4 switches the color mode to 1-bit by itself.

Why the TIFF is large

TIFF stores a bitmap of every page. An uncompressed 300 DPI color Letter page is about 25 MB, so lower the DPI, switch to greyscale or black and white, or use CCITT Group 4 for text scans. For lossless images on screen, PDF to PNG is smaller.

How it works

Your browser draws each page at the chosen resolution and a TIFF encoder on your device writes the file with the compression you chose. The PDF is never uploaded.

What people use it for, and when it will not work

Fax gateways, court e-filing, records and document management systems, printers and print shops, archives that require TIFF.

  • The text is no longer text. Each page becomes an image; run PDF OCR on the PDF if you need searchable text.
  • One PDF at a time. Convert further files separately.

Questions and answers

How do I convert PDF to TIFF?
Add the PDF, set the DPI, compression and color mode and press Convert.
Is PDF to TIFF free?
Yes, with no account, no watermark and no limit on files.
Can I get all pages in one multi-page TIFF?
Yes. Tick Save as multi-page TIFF. Otherwise you get a ZIP with one TIFF per page.
What DPI should I use?
300 for print and archives, 200 to 300 for fax and document systems, 150 for screens.
Which compression should I choose?
LZW or Deflate for any content, CCITT Group 4 for black and white scans, JPEG for photo pages.
How do I make a black and white TIFF for fax or e-filing?
Set Black & White (1-bit), CCITT Group 4 and 200 or 300 DPI.
Why is my TIFF so large?
TIFF stores a bitmap per page. Lower the DPI, use greyscale or black and white, or CCITT Group 4.
Is my PDF uploaded?
No. The conversion runs in your browser and the file never leaves your device.
