How to make bingo cards

  1. Add your words

    Type one word or phrase per line, pick one of ten themes, or choose numbers 1 to 75.

  2. Pick the grid

    Small grids for young children, 5×5 for the classic game. The free center square is optional.

  3. Set how many cards

    Every card in the set is different. Choose 1, 2 or 4 cards per page.

  4. Print the set

    Print the cards, the caller sheet and the calling cards, or save one PDF for later.

What you get

This bingo card generator makes a whole set of printable bingo cards in one go: every card different, with a caller sheet to tick off and calling cards to draw from a hat. It is free, needs no account and works in your browser, so your word lists are never uploaded.

Custom bingo cards with your own words

Choose My words and type one word or short phrase per line: names of guests, vocabulary from this week’s lesson, things to spot on a walk. A 5×5 card with a free space needs at least 24 different entries, a 4×4 card 16 and a 3×3 card 8. More entries than squares make the game longer and the cards more varied, because each card then gets its own selection.

Long phrases are fine. Text shrinks and wraps onto a second or third line to stay inside its square, and a single very long word is split with a hyphen only as a last resort. Accented letters and Cyrillic and Greek text print correctly.

Number bingo cards (1 to 75)

Choose Numbers for the classic game. On 5×5 cards each column has its own range, with the letters B, I, N, G and O on top: B is 1 to 15, I is 16 to 30, N is 31 to 45, G is 46 to 60 and O is 61 to 75. The caller announces “B 7” or “G 52”, and players look in one column only. Smaller grids use 1 to 45 (3×3) or 1 to 60 (4×4) in the same column pattern.

The free space is the center square that counts as already marked, so lines through the middle need one fewer call. Turn it off for a longer game. A 4×4 card has no center square, so the option is off for that size.

Make a set of unique cards

Set the number of cards you need, one per player plus a few spare, and the generator makes them all different: when there are enough words, no two cards share the same set of words; otherwise no two share the same layout. Each card carries a small number such as “Card 7 of 30”, which helps you check a winning card against the caller sheet. Press New set of cards to shuffle a fresh set.

Caller sheet and calling cards

The caller sheet lists every word or number with a box to tick as you call it, so you can check a shout of “Bingo!” in seconds. The calling cards are the same words, 24 to a page, with dashed lines to cut along: fold them into a bowl or hat and draw one at a time. Both are optional.

Ideas: themes for parties and classrooms

Ten ready word lists are built in: animals, fruit and vegetables, weather and seasons, space, first words for reading, baby shower, bridal shower, winter holidays, Halloween and road trip. Teachers use word bingo for spelling, vocabulary in a new language, times tables (write the sums on the calling cards and the answers on the cards) and history dates. At parties it works for guests’ names, film quotes or things that happen during the evening.

How it works

The cards are laid out in your browser: words are shuffled with a random seed, each new card is checked against the ones already made, and text is measured with the same font used for printing, so what fits on screen fits on paper. Print uses your browser’s print dialog; Download PDF saves cards, caller sheet and calling cards in one file.

What people use it for, and when it will not work

Birthday parties, baby and bridal showers, classroom review games, family game nights, care homes, team building, travel games for children.

Questions and answers

Are all the cards in a set different?
Yes. Every card is checked against the others. With more words than squares, no two cards have the same set of words; with exactly enough words, no two have the same layout.
How many squares are on a bingo card?
A classic card is 5×5, 25 squares, with a free space in the center, so 24 to fill. This generator also makes 4×4 (16) and 3×3 (9) cards.
How many cards can I make?
As many as you need. Thirty cards take a moment; even several hundred work, though printing them takes a while.
Can I print bingo cards for free?
Yes. The cards, caller sheet and calling cards are free to print and use at home, in class or at events, with no account and no watermark.
How do I play bingo with my own words?
Give each player a card. The caller draws calling cards one at a time and reads the word; players mark it if it is on their card. The first to complete a row, column or diagonal shouts “Bingo!”.
What is the free space?
The center square of a 3×3 or 5×5 card that counts as marked from the start. It makes lines through the middle easier. You can turn it off for a longer game.
Can I make bingo cards with pictures?
Not yet. Cards hold words, phrases and numbers. For young children who cannot read yet, short words with a 3×3 grid and large text work well.
How do I call bingo?
Shuffle the calling cards or numbers, draw one, read it out and tick it on the caller sheet. When someone calls bingo, check their marked squares against the ticked items.
