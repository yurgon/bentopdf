How to sign a PDF

  1. Open the PDF

    The document opens in a viewer on this page. It is not uploaded.

  2. Create your signature

    Draw it with a mouse or finger, type your name, or use a picture of your signature.

  3. Place it and add the date

    Drag the signature where it belongs and resize it. Add text for the date, your printed name or initials.

  4. Save the signed PDF

    Download it to your device, ready to send back.

Where Here Digital Sign PDF

To sign a PDF is to put your signature on it without printing and scanning. This tool lets you draw, type or upload your signature and place it on the page, together with the date or your initials. It runs in your browser, so contracts and forms are never uploaded to a signing service.

Draw, type or upload a signature

Draw your signature with a mouse, trackpad or finger on a phone; type your name and it is set in a handwriting style; or use a photo or scan of your real signature. Need a cleaner image first? Make one with the Signature Generator and upload it here.

Add the date, your name and initials

Most forms ask for more than a signature. Use the text tool to type the date, your printed name or initials on any page, and move each item to the right line. When you are done, turn on flatten so the signature and text become part of the page and cannot be moved by the recipient.

Is an electronic signature legally valid?

In the United States, the ESIGN Act and UETA give electronic signatures the same legal effect as handwritten ones for most agreements, and the EU has similar rules. Some documents, such as wills or certain court papers, still need a handwritten or certified signature. When a recipient asks for a signature that proves the file was not changed, use Digital Sign PDF with a certificate. This is general information, not legal advice.

How it works

The PDF opens in a PDF.js viewer on this page. Your signature is created and placed in that viewer, then written into the document as an annotation, or into the page itself when you flatten. All of it happens in your browser; neither the document nor your signature is sent to a server.

What people use it for, and when it will not work

Leases and rental applications, job offers and onboarding forms, permission slips, invoices and delivery notes, consent forms and everyday contracts.

  • Sending for others to sign is not offered: each person signs the file themselves and sends it on.
  • An electronic signature does not show tampering. When that matters, use a certificate-based digital signature.
  • Digitally signed PDFs lose their existing signature if you add anything to them.
