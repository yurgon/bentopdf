Converting a PDF to Excel turns the tables in it into rows and columns you can sort, filter and add up. Copying a table out of a PDF by hand usually lands everything in one column. Here the tables are found automatically, in your browser, so bank statements and invoices are never uploaded.

Extract tables from a PDF

Each page is searched for tables: ruled grids and neatly aligned columns. Every table found goes on its own sheet, named by its number and page, such as Table 2 (Page 3), so you can match it to the PDF. A PDF with a single table gives a workbook with one sheet.

Bank statements and invoices

Statements, invoices, price lists and reports exported from software convert best, because their text and lines are stored exactly. A table that runs over several pages comes out as one sheet per page; paste the sheets together in Excel if you need one list. Check dates and amounts once, especially where cells were merged in the PDF.

Open the result in Google Sheets or Numbers

The file is a standard XLSX workbook. Open it in Excel, upload it to Google Sheets with File, Import, or open it in Apple Numbers or LibreOffice Calc.

How it works

Tables are detected with MuPDF, a PDF engine running in your browser, which looks at the text positions and lines on each page and works out rows and columns. The cells are written into an XLSX workbook in memory and saved to your device. The first time, the engine (about 39 MB) is downloaded and kept by your browser. Nothing is sent to a server.

What people use it for, and when it will not work

Bank and card statements for budgeting or taxes, invoices for bookkeeping, price lists and catalogs, research data from papers, and reports that only exist as PDF.

  • Scanned PDFs do not work: a scan has no text, so no table can be found.
  • Only tables are converted, not the text around them.
  • Formulas, colors and fonts are not carried over; cells hold the values.
  • Unusual layouts, such as tables without lines and uneven spacing, may split or merge columns.
