How to convert PowerPoint to PDF

  1. Add your presentations

    PPTX, PPT or ODP, one or several. They are opened on your device.

  2. Convert

    The first time, the converter downloads once, about 77 MB. After that it starts at once.

  3. Save the PDF

    One presentation is saved as a PDF; several come together in a ZIP.

What you get

What carries over from the presentation

Converting PowerPoint to PDF freezes your slides: anyone can open them on a phone, a laptop or a printer, without PowerPoint and without fonts changing. This converter runs LibreOffice inside your browser, so your presentation is never uploaded, and it works with PPTX, older PPT and ODP files.

PPTX, PPT and ODP

Modern PowerPoint files (.pptx), older ones (.ppt) and LibreOffice or Google Slides exports (.odp) all convert the same way. To convert a Google Slides deck, download it as PowerPoint or ODP first, then add it here. Keynote files need to be exported from Keynote as PowerPoint.

Fonts and layout

Fonts embedded in the presentation are used as they are. If a slide uses a font that is not embedded and is not available in the converter, a similar font is used, which can shift a line of text. Check the PDF once, and if a slide moved, embed fonts when saving in PowerPoint (Save options, Embed fonts) and convert again.

Large presentations and several files

Add as many decks as you like; they are converted one after another and saved in a ZIP. Big image-heavy decks give big PDFs; run the result through Compress PDF before you email it.

How it works

The converter is LibreOffice, the open source office suite, compiled to run inside your browser. It opens each presentation, lays out every slide and prints it to PDF, all on your device. The first time, its engine (about 77 MB) is downloaded and kept by your browser, so later conversions start straight away, even offline.

What people use it for, and when it will not work

Sending slides to a client or teacher, handing in coursework, printing handouts, sharing a deck that must not be edited, and uploading to portals that only take PDF.

  • Animations, video and sound cannot be shown in a PDF.
  • Speaker notes are not included; the PDF has one slide per page.
  • Password-protected presentations must be opened and saved without the password first.
  • On older phones the first download and large decks can be slow; a computer is faster.

Questions and answers

How do I convert PowerPoint to PDF?
Add the PPTX or PPT file on this page and press Convert to PDF. The PDF is saved to your device.
Is converting PowerPoint to PDF free?
Yes, with no account, no watermark and no limit on files.
Will the PDF look like my presentation?
Yes, one slide per page with the same layout. Fonts that are not embedded may be replaced by similar ones.
How do I convert PPT to PDF on my phone?
Open this page in your phone's browser, add the file from Files or Drive and convert. It works without an app.
Why is the first conversion slow?
The converter, about 77 MB, is downloaded once. It is kept in your browser, so later conversions start at once.
Are my files uploaded?
No. Conversion runs in your browser and your presentation never leaves your device.
Can I convert several presentations at once?
Yes. Add them all; the PDFs are saved together in a ZIP.
