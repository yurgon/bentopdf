How to convert a PDF to JPG

  1. Add the PDF

    Choose one PDF file. Locked files ask for their password.

  2. Set the quality

    90% looks the same as the page and keeps files small. Go lower for tiny previews, higher for print.

  3. Convert and save

    Press Convert. A one-page PDF gives one JPG; longer PDFs come as a ZIP with a JPG per page.

What you get

Converting PDF to JPG turns each page into an ordinary picture. Pictures open in any photo app, go into a presentation or a chat, and can be posted where PDFs are not allowed. This converter draws the pages in your browser, so the document never leaves your computer or phone.

Quality and file size

The slider sets JPEG compression. At 90%, the default, text edges look clean and an A4 page is typically 200 to 600 KB. Lower values shrink the file but add blur and blotches around letters; below 50% they are easy to see. Pages are drawn at twice the normal screen size, 144 pixels per inch, which is sharp on phones and fine for printing at home. For a bigger image, use PDF to PNG, which goes up to three times.

Convert only one page

The tool converts every page. If you need just one or two, take them out first with Extract pages, then convert that small PDF. If what you want is the photos placed inside a PDF rather than whole pages, use Extract images: it saves the original pictures at their own resolution.

JPG or PNG?

JPG is smaller and right for pages with photos. PNG is lossless: every pixel stays exact, which keeps small text, line drawings and charts crisp, but files are larger. For a scanned page or a brochure, choose JPG; for a spreadsheet, a diagram or text you will zoom into, choose PNG.

How it works

Each page is drawn onto a canvas in your browser, the same way a PDF viewer draws it on screen, and saved as a JPG. White areas stay white; there is no transparency in JPG. Everything happens on your device, and the images are saved straight to it.

What people use it for, and when it will not work

Posting a flyer or a menu on social media, putting a chart into slides, sending a page in a messenger, making a thumbnail of a document, uploading to a site that only accepts images.

  • One PDF at a time. Convert several files one after another.
  • Text in the image can no longer be searched or copied. Keep the PDF for that.
  • Very long documents of hundreds of pages make a big ZIP and take a while on a phone.
