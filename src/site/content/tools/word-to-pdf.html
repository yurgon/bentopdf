How to convert a Word document to PDF

  1. Add your documents

    Choose one Word file or several. DOC, ODT and RTF work too.

  2. Let it convert

    The first time, the converter loads into your browser and takes a little while. After that it starts at once.

  3. Save the PDF

    One document gives one PDF; several come as a ZIP. Check the pages before you send them.

What you get

Word documents look slightly different on every computer, depending on the fonts and the version of the program. A PDF looks the same everywhere and prints exactly as you see it, which is why applications, invoices and contracts are sent as PDF. This page converts Word to PDF in your browser, with no Office licence and no upload.

Why the first conversion takes a moment

The conversion is done by LibreOffice, a full office suite, running inside your browser. The first time you convert, its engine is downloaded and stored; this takes from a few seconds to a minute depending on your connection. From then on it starts straight away and works without an internet connection.

Fonts and layout

Text, headings, tables, images, headers and footers, page numbers and hyperlinks are kept. The one thing to watch is fonts. Microsoft fonts such as Calibri and Cambria are replaced with free fonts of exactly the same width, so lines break in the same places. Other fonts that the converter does not have are replaced with similar ones, and a long paragraph may move a line. Check the pages before sending; for documents where every line matters, export the PDF from Word itself.

Which files work

DOCX from Word 2007 and later, old DOC files, ODT from LibreOffice and OpenOffice, and RTF. For other office files there are separate converters: Excel to PDF, PowerPoint to PDF and Pages to PDF.

How it works

Each document is opened by LibreOffice in your browser, laid out into pages exactly as LibreOffice would print it, and exported as PDF. Several files are converted one after another and packed into a ZIP. The documents stay in your browser's memory and are never sent to a server.

What people use it for, and when it will not work

CVs and cover letters, invoices and quotes, school and university papers, contracts to sign, documents for official portals that only accept PDF.

  • Password-protected Word files cannot be opened; remove the password in Word first.
  • Tracked changes and comments appear as they would when printed. Accept or delete them first if they should not be in the PDF.
  • Macros and embedded videos do not carry over; a PDF cannot run them.
  • Phones with little memory may struggle with the converter. A computer is more reliable.

Questions and answers

How do I convert Word to PDF for free?
Add the DOCX on this page and press Convert to PDF. The PDF is saved to your device. No account, no watermark and no Office licence needed.
Will the formatting change?
Layout, tables, images and links are kept. Fonts the converter does not have are replaced with similar ones, which can move a line, so check the pages before sending.
Can I convert DOC, ODT and RTF files too?
Yes. Old Word DOC files, ODT from LibreOffice and OpenOffice, and RTF all work.
Can I convert several documents at once?
Yes. Add them all; you get a ZIP with one PDF for each document.
Are my documents uploaded?
No. The converter runs inside your browser and the PDFs are saved straight to your device.
Do hyperlinks still work in the PDF?
Yes. Links in the text stay clickable in the PDF.
Why is the first conversion slow?
The LibreOffice engine is downloaded the first time. It is kept in your browser, so later conversions start straight away, even offline.
How do I turn the PDF back into Word?
Use PDF to Word. It rebuilds paragraphs and tables into an editable DOCX.
