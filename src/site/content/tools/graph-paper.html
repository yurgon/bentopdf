How to make printable graph paper

  1. Pick the paper type

    Square grid, millimeter paper, dot grid, isometric or lined writing paper.

  2. Set the square size

    Type any size in millimeters or inches, or tap a common one. Add a bold line every few squares if you like.

  3. Check the preview

    The preview shows the real page. Change the line color, thickness or margins and it updates at once.

  4. Print or download

    Press Print and choose Actual size or 100%, or save a PDF to print later or share.

What you get

Graph paper, also called grid paper or squared paper, is paper printed with a grid of equal squares. Here you set the square size, the line color and thickness and the margins, see the page as it will print, and print it on your own printer. No sign-up and nothing is uploaded.

Printable graph paper: 1/4 inch, 5 mm, 1 cm and 1 inch

Common graph paper square sizes and what they are used for
Square size Squares on a page Used for
1/4 inch (6.35 mm) About 30 × 40 on Letter The US standard: math, charts, floor plans
1/5 inch (5.08 mm) About 38 × 50 on Letter Engineering and science notebooks
5 mm About 38 × 55 on A4 The European standard school grid
1 cm About 19 × 27 on A4 Young children, large plots, pixel art
1/2 inch and 1 inch 15 × 20 or 7 × 10 on Letter Early math, games, quilting plans
1 or 2 mm Hundreds per row Precise drawing, see millimeter paper

The grid is centered on the page and ends on a whole square, so every square is complete. With bold lines every 4, 5 or 10 squares, it also ends on a bold line.

Millimeter paper with bold lines every 5 or 10 squares

Millimeter paper has a 1 mm grid with a medium line every 5 mm and a bold line every centimeter, in three weights so you can count squares without a ruler. It is the paper for graphs in physics and engineering, technical sketches and sewing patterns. Choose it in Paper type: the sizes are fixed and the line thickness slider sets all three weights together.

Choose line color, thickness and margins

Gray lines guide the hand without competing with what you draw. Light blue copies traditional engineering paper and stays out of photocopies; black is for grids that must show on scans. Thickness runs from a faint 0.1 point to a strong 1.5 points. Margins of 10 mm keep the grid inside what nearly every printer can reach; set 0 if your printer prints edge to edge.

Print on A4 or Letter: will the squares be exact?

Yes, if nothing scales the page. The grid is drawn at real size, so in the print dialog choose Actual size or 100%, not Fit to page or Shrink to fit. To check your printer, print the alignment sheet of the printer test page: its ruler should measure exactly 100 mm. Inkjet paper can stretch a fraction of a percent as it dries, which is invisible over a few squares.

Dot grid, isometric and lined paper

  • Dot grid puts a small dot where grid lines would cross. Bullet journals and sketch notes use it because the dots guide without boxing in.
  • Isometric paper is a grid of equilateral triangles with vertical lines, for drawing 3D objects and cubes at 30 degrees.
  • Lined paper comes in wide ruled (11/32 inch, 8.7 mm), college ruled (9/32 inch, 7.1 mm) and narrow ruled (1/4 inch), with an optional red margin line and space for a name.

How it works

The page computes every line from your settings and draws it as a vector line, not as a picture of a grid, so it stays sharp at any printer resolution and the PDF is tiny. Print sends the page at real size through your browser’s print dialog; Download PDF saves the same sheet. It all happens in your browser.

What people use it for, and when it will not work

Math homework and graphing, science lab notes, floor plans and garden layouts, knitting and cross stitch charts, pixel art, handwriting practice, bullet journals, game maps.

  • Squares smaller than about 1 mm are refused: most printers cannot print them clearly.
  • Edge to edge grids need a printer that supports borderless printing; others leave a blank strip of 3 to 5 mm.
  • Coordinate planes with numbered axes, polar and logarithmic paper are not here yet.

Questions and answers

What size squares does standard graph paper have?
In the United States the standard is 1/4 inch, four squares per inch. In Europe and most other countries it is 5 mm. Engineering paper often uses 1/5 inch.
What is 1/4 inch graph paper?
Graph paper with four squares to the inch, each 6.35 mm wide. On a Letter page with 10 mm margins that makes about 30 squares across and 40 down.
How do I print graph paper?
Pick the paper type and square size above, press Print and set the scale to Actual size or 100% in the print dialog. Or press Download PDF and print the file later.
Can I print graph paper on A4?
Yes. Choose A4 in the Paper option. Any square size works on either A4 or Letter; only the number of squares that fit changes.
What is the difference between graph paper and grid paper?
None in everyday use: both mean paper printed with a grid of squares. Squared paper and quad paper are other names for it.
What is graph paper used for?
Plotting graphs in math and science, drawing to scale, floor plans, sewing and knitting charts, pixel art and keeping handwriting and numbers in neat columns.
How do I make my own graph paper?
Set your own square size, line thickness, color and margins on this page. The preview shows exactly what will print, and you can save it as a PDF to reuse.
Will the printed squares be the exact size?
Yes, when you print at Actual size or 100%. Check with the 100 mm ruler on our printer test page if you need to be sure your printer does not scale.
