How to make flashcards

  1. Paste terms and definitions

    One card per line: the term, a dash, the definition. Or paste two columns from a spreadsheet.

  2. Choose 4, 6 or 8 per sheet

    Bigger cards for young children, eight small ones to save paper.

  3. Print on both sides

    Pages alternate fronts and backs. Each back is mirrored to land behind its front.

  4. Cut along the lines

    Dashed lines mark every card edge, the same on both sides.

What you get

This flashcard maker turns a list of terms and definitions into printable flashcards with the answer on the back. Backs are placed so that a two-sided print puts each definition right behind its term, and dashed lines show where to cut. It is free, needs no account and works in your browser, so your list is never uploaded.

Paste terms and definitions

Type one card per line: the term, then a dash, a colon or a tab, then the definition. To make flashcards from a spreadsheet, select two columns in Excel, Google Sheets or Numbers, copy and paste: each row becomes a card. The same works for a vocabulary list (word and translation), a quiz (question and answer) or times tables (sum and result). A line without a separator makes a card with an empty back.

Printing double-sided flashcards

The PDF alternates pages: fronts, backs, fronts, backs. When paper turns over its long edge, the left card on the front ends up behind the right card on the back, so the backs are mirrored: the definition for the top left term is printed top right. Margins are the same on every side, so the cards line up even if your printer shifts the page slightly.

  1. Choose how your printer flips the paper: Long edge is the usual setting.
  2. Print with two-sided printing turned on, flipping on the same edge.
  3. Check one card: the small number in the corner is the same on both sides.

No two-sided printer? Print the odd pages first, put the stack back in the tray printed side down (try one sheet to see which way your printer feeds), then print the even pages.

Dashed cut lines run along every card edge and to the margins, on both sides, so you can cut from either side with scissors or a paper trimmer. Long definitions wrap and shrink to stay on their card.

Flashcards for students

Printable flashcards suit spelling tests, foreign language vocabulary, science and history terms, math facts and exam revision. Put the definition on the front to practice recall the other way around, add the set name so mixed stacks can be sorted, and use heavier paper or card stock if your printer takes it.

How it works

The cards are laid out in your browser. Text is measured with the print font so it fits the card on paper as it does on screen. Print opens your browser’s print dialog at real size; Download PDF saves every page in the right order for two-sided printing on A4 or Letter.

What people use it for, and when it will not work

Vocabulary, exam revision, language classes, homeschool lessons, speech cue cards, training for new staff.

  • Studying online with spaced repetition is not part of this tool: the cards are for printing.
  • Pictures on cards are not supported yet.
  • Chinese, Japanese, Korean and emoji characters are not in the print font. Latin, Cyrillic and Greek letters work.

Questions and answers

Is this flashcard maker free?
Yes. Make and print as many cards as you like, with no account and no watermark.
How do I print double-sided flashcards so the backs line up?
Turn on two-sided printing and flip on the long edge, the usual setting. The backs are already mirrored, so each definition lands behind its term. If your printer flips on the short edge, choose Short edge in the tool.
What if my printer cannot print on both sides?
Print the odd pages, put the sheets back in the tray to print on the blank side, then print the even pages. Try one sheet first to see which way your printer feeds paper.
How do I make flashcards from a spreadsheet?
Select two columns, terms and definitions, copy them and paste into the box. Each row becomes one card.
How many flashcards fit on a page?
Choose 4, 6 or 8 cards per sheet. Eight is a common size for vocabulary cards, four suits young children and large text.
Can I use this as a flash card template?
Yes. Leave the definitions empty to print blank backs, or type the terms only and write the answers by hand.
Can I put pictures on the cards?
Not yet. Cards hold text, which shrinks and wraps to fit.
Is my list uploaded?
No. The cards are made on your device. Nothing you type is sent anywhere.
