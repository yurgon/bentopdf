# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

People who arrive from a search with one concrete job: print a PDF as a booklet, black out a line in a contract, make bingo cards for a class, turn a book into EPUB for their e-reader, sign a form. Mostly English-speaking (US first), on a laptop at home or at work, often on a phone. Teachers, parents, office workers, students, readers. They are not PDF experts and do not want to become ones. They give a page a few seconds to prove it can do the job, and they leave as soon as it is done.

A second audience is AI assistants that use the same tools through an MCP connection; they never see the design, but the documentation pages they read are part of the site.

## Product Purpose

A free site of PDF tools (a fork of BentoPDF) where every tool has its own page that both does the job in the top half and fully answers the search query below it: instructions, an example of the result, how it works, limits, questions and answers. Files never leave the visitor's device. Two sections nobody else has in this form: "For printing" (booklet, printer test page, graph paper, bingo cards, tracing worksheets, poster) and "For e-readers" (PDF to EPUB and other book formats).

Success: the page is found in search, the visitor finishes the task on the first try, and trusts the site enough to come back.

## Positioning

Every tool runs on your device, with no upload, no account and no limits, and every page explains the job better than the first three search results.

## Brand Personality

Calm, precise, generous. Feels expensive and professional, like a product made by a small strong team (Linear, Stripe), not a pile of free utilities. Plain, direct copy that speaks from the visitor's side of the screen. Confidence comes from clarity and honest limits, not from adjectives.

## Anti-references

BentoPDF itself and the typical PDF-tool sites (Smallpdf, iLovePDF, PDF24, Sejda): loud colored tool tiles, a wall of identical icon cards, upload banners, upsell modals, ads. No stock illustrations, no gradients for decoration, no icons used as ornament, no attention-seeking animation. Competitor names never appear in the brand or the visuals.

## Design Principles

- One main thing per screen: the work area. Everything else waits below it.
- Show, don't claim: previews, diagrams of the real result, a sample file to try, rather than "powerful and easy".
- Privacy is visible, not promised: the page shows what happens on the device.
- Honest limits are part of the design: what the tool will not do is stated as plainly as what it does.
- One component vocabulary across every page, so a visitor who learned one tool knows them all.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA: text contrast at least 4.5:1, everything reachable and operable from the keyboard with a visible focus state, reduced-motion respected, layouts that work from a 360px phone to a wide screen. Light and dark themes.
